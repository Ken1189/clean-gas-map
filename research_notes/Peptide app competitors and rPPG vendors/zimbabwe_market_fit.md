# Market fit for a peptide/GLP-1 safety app with camera vitals in Zimbabwe and Africa

Research date: 27 Nov 2026. Method: web search (about 20 queries). Direct page fetches to spotlightnsp.co.za and businessdailyafrica.com were blocked by this session's network allowlist, so several findings rest on search-result summaries of the cited pages rather than full-text reads. Figures marked "secondary" come from blogs or aggregators and should be checked before external use.

## GLP-1 demand and access in Zimbabwe and Southern Africa

### Takeaway
Zimbabwe has a small diagnosed diabetes market. The IDF estimate is about 106,500 adults, or 1.5% age-standardised prevalence, and about 69% of cases are undiagnosed. Branded GLP-1 access appears formally limited or absent, and there are counterfeit warnings. South Africa is the regional GLP-1 hub: Wegovy launched there in 2025, a Novo authorised copy (Extensior) and Sun Pharma's generic (Semagard) launched in July–August 2026, and more than 12 generic applications are pending. Zimbabweans are therefore likely to source GLP-1s cross-border from South Africa, through the diaspora or informally. That makes a safety, authenticity and dosing-support angle more relevant than a US-style "compounded peptide protocol" tracker.

### Inferences
- The addressable Zimbabwe GLP-1 user base is likely small, affluent, urban and diaspora-linked, buying in South Africa or via informal channels. The core product value therefore shifts to authenticity checks (batch/pen verification, counterfeit red flags), dose titration support across brand switches (Ozempic → Extensior → Semagard), side-effect triage, and safe storage in a hot climate with load-shedding (cold-chain).
- Generics flowing from India and South Africa will make semaglutide cheaper and more widespread in 2027 and later. Being positioned for multi-brand and generic semaglutide is more important than tirzepatide support.
- Under-diagnosis (69%) suggests a wider funnel: screening and BMI or BP awareness for pre-diabetes and obesity, with GLP-1 safety as a premium module.

## Smartphone and connectivity

### Takeaway
Zimbabwe is overwhelmingly Android, at about 87–88% of mobile web traffic. Mobile internet penetration is around 85–87%, and WhatsApp is the dominant platform. Data costs about US$1/GB on Econet's current bundles. Google Play supports merchant registration and paid apps in Zimbabwe. Build Android-first, lightweight and data-frugal, with WhatsApp as a core channel.

### Inferences
- An iOS-first US competitor misses about 88% of Zimbabwe's mobile users. Android-first with a small APK size, offline-capable logging and low-res rPPG capture is required.
- A 30-second camera vitals scan should run on-device. Uploading video at about US$1/GB would cost users money and erode trust.
- Because consumer card penetration is low (see Payments), Google Play billing will reach few users. Selling digital subscriptions via EcoCash inside a Play-distributed app risks policy violation, so a PWA or web checkout (Paynow) for subscriptions, or a B2B2C model, is a practical workaround. This needs legal and policy review.
- Zero-rating deals with Econet or NetOne, as precedent telehealth players have pursued, could be a meaningful adoption lever.

- NetOne and Telecel 2026 data pricing were not retrieved. The current WhatsApp share of traffic is unquantified.
- No sources on PWA usage or viability in Zimbabwe specifically.

## Payments

### Takeaway
Zimbabwe is heavily dollarised: an estimated 60–85% of transactions are in USD, depending on the source. EcoCash dominates mobile money with more than 86% share. InnBucks and O'Mari are the challengers, and OneMoney is a distant third. Aggregators such as Paynow bundle EcoCash, OneMoney, InnBucks, O'Mari, Visa, Mastercard and ZimSwitch through one API. Diaspora remittances (US$2.45bn in 2025), now led by the UK ahead of South Africa, make a "diaspora pays for family member's plan" model plausible.

### Inferences
- Price in USD, accept EcoCash, InnBucks and O'Mari via an aggregator such as Paynow, and keep ZiG optional. Short billing cycles (weekly or monthly micro-subscriptions, or pay-per-scan) match prepaid-airtime behaviour better than annual subscriptions.
- A diaspora-gifting flow, where a UK or South African card pays for a relative in Zimbabwe, is likely to monetise better than local consumers alone. GLP-1 users with South African supply chains overlap with this cohort.
- Google Play billing should be secondary. The primary revenue route is web or mobile-money checkout, or B2B contracts (medical aids, clinics, employers).

## Digital health precedents and rPPG

### Takeaway
WhatsApp-first health services have proven scale in Africa. MomConnect in South Africa reports more than 4.7M users and WhatsApp engagement about 10× SMS. Zambia's DawaMom serves Zimbabwean users via WhatsApp. Lessons from these services: offer an SMS or offline fallback, minimise registration friction, and expect data-cost barriers. rPPG accuracy on darker skin is contested, and I found no African field deployment of smartphone rPPG.

### Cited Findings
- MomConnect (South Africa): over 4.7M users and over 422k monthly active users, with WhatsApp engagement about 10× higher than SMS (Reach Digital Health). Another source gives about 450k monthly users. — [Reach Digital Health](https://www.reachdigitalhealth.org/resources/momconnect-chatbot); [Ada](https://about.ada.com/?p=11279)
- MomConnect's lessons: WhatsApp was offered from late 2017 and made the default in September 2018. Older mothers saw WhatsApp as unreliable without data, so the team built self-service SMS/WhatsApp switching. One in four users dropped out during registration, and SMS will remain necessary for some users for years. — [measured.design](https://measured.design/?p=1246); [BMJ Global Health via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5922483); [DIAL](https://dial.global/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/MomConnect.pdf)
- WhatsApp's vendor case study claims higher engagement and lower cost than SMS. It is vendor-supplied. — [WhatsApp Business](https://business.whatsapp.com/resources/success-stories/momconnect)
- DawaMom (Zambia-based DawaHealth) is a WhatsApp maternal-health tool serving Zimbabwean users. It has more than 20,000 users and targets 50,000 by end-2026 (company figures). — [Gavi](https://www.gavi.org/vaccineswork/zimbabwe-ai-midwife-making-pregnancy-safer-underserved-women); [AllAfrica, Oct 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202610190008.html)
- Nyamukuta, an AI chatbot for pregnant women in Epworth, launched alongside blood-pressure machines, a hybrid of digital tool and physical vitals. — [Gavi](https://www.gavi.org/vaccineswork/zimbabwe-ai-midwife-making-pregnancy-safer-underserved-women)
- rPPG and skin tone: a 2025 npj Digital Medicine brief says most public rPPG datasets are demographically biased, and very dark skin can exceed RGB sensor dynamic range. One study found prior methods biased against darker-skinned subjects. — [PMC10568358](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10568358); [arXiv 2010.12769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12769v2)
- Counter-evidence: a 2021 medRxiv smartphone heart-rate study found no significant MAPE difference across skin-tone groups. A 2023 Cureus study (n=90) found no skin-tone effect. Google's passive smartphone heart-rate monitoring achieved MAPE under 10% across light, medium and dark groups. — [medRxiv](https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.03.08.21252408v1.full); [Cureus](https://assets.cureus.com/uploads/original_article/pdf/150278/20230912-4180-vpq056.pdf); [arXiv 2503.03783](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03783v3)
- Wearable PPG still shows variation for darker skin, per a 2025 review. — [PMC12592569](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12592569/)

### Inferences
- Validation on Zimbabwean or Fitzpatrick V–VI users is a credibility prerequisite and a differentiator against US-built trackers. Heart rate is likely defensible, while BP, SpO2 and HRV from the camera are riskier claims.
- A WhatsApp companion (reminders, dose logs, side-effect check-ins, escalation) likely drives retention better than a standalone app. The camera scan stays in-app or in a PWA.

### Gaps
- No African field deployment or validation of smartphone rPPG found (vendors such as Binah.ai, NuraLogix or Shen.ai in Africa were not covered).
- MomConnect's independent evaluation findings (IDInsight, RCT) were not retrieved.
