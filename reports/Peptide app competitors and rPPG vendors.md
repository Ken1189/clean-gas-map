# Safety-first design beats Pep AI in Africa

No peptide or GLP-1 tracker on the market today combines symptom triage, counterfeit checks, dose-error detection and camera vitals. That is the opening PeptiSafe Africa should take. Pep AI, the fastest-growing peptide app of 2026, and Shotsy, the GLP-1 category leader, both win on logging breadth, polish and US marketing. Neither has red-flag escalation, authenticity verification or vitals capture, and both are priced and built for US iPhone users, while Zimbabwe is about 88% Android, dollarised, WhatsApp-first and paid for through EcoCash. Shen.AI is the right rPPG vendor for now. It is the only one with published pricing, on-device processing, a Web SDK that fits a PWA, a CE Class IIa certificate that covers blood pressure, and tools to read a cuff's screen and calibrate against it. Its face-only blood pressure is still too inaccurate for safety alerts, though: the vendor's own systolic error is 10 mmHg, and the only African field study of a comparable CE-marked tool detected almost no hypertension in very dark skin. PeptiSafe should therefore alert on heart-rate trends and cuff-confirmed blood pressure, never on face BP alone. The biggest risks are regulatory rather than competitive. Dose calculators, camera blood-pressure claims, influencer medicine promotion and any link to research-peptide vendors each have documented enforcement behind them. PeptiSafe can win by being the compliant, offline, Android-first safety layer, sold through mobile money and WhatsApp, and by not trying to match Pep AI on tracking features.

## Pep AI grew to six-figure monthly revenue with no safety layer

Pep AI ("Pep AI: Peptide GLP-1 Tracker", App Store ID 6758682710, Play package `app.replit.pepai`) is the competitor everyone in the category now measures against. It is also very new. A 23-year-old marketing student, Cedric Roberge, built it on Replit in about a week, and it is sold through his entity Zode Development LLC ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pep-ai/id6758682710); [Replit on X](https://x.com/Replit/status/2094545898063724619)). **Version 1.0 shipped on 17 February 2026.** The founder seeded a waitlist of about 300 people through peptide Reddit and Discord communities ([Sofarbot](https://www.sofarbot.com/stories/pep-ai-cedric-roberge-30000-monthly-revenue); [MRR Story](https://www.mrrstory.com/stories/he-got-his-first-100-users-from-one-reddit-post-now-hit-60k-mrr)). From there the founder's own revenue claims climb steeply: **over $96,000 a month by June 2026**, about **$130k a month** in Replit's telling, and **200,000 downloads with nearly $1 million in total revenue** about eight months after launch ([Founderbase](https://founderbase.ai/interviews/pep-ai); [Replit on X](https://x.com/Replit/status/2094545898063724619); [Manny Bernabe on X](https://x.com/MannyBernabe/status/2113340534060367876)). None of these figures is audited, and one independent analysis says the evidence supports gross monthly revenue better than a clean recurring-revenue figure ([Sofarbot](https://www.sofarbot.com/stories/pep-ai-cedric-roberge-30000-monthly-revenue)).

Its ratings are solid but smaller than sometimes claimed. The US App Store shows **4.7–4.8 stars from roughly 3,700–4,050 ratings**, and the vendor itself claims "4,000+ ratings" as of November 2026. Google Play shows about **4.6 stars from 300–350 reviews** ([App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pep-ai-peptide-glp-1-tracker/id6758682710?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone); [pepaiapp.com](https://pepaiapp.com/); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.replit.pepai)). No source supports the figure of roughly 33,000 ratings that circulates for Pep AI. It probably comes from confusion with Shotsy, which claims 34,000+ App Store ratings ([Shotsy](https://shotsyapp.com/?amp=&amp=)).

On features, Pep AI has essentially everything a tracker can have. It covers inventory and vials, a reconstitution calculator with an interactive syringe for U-100 and U-40 syringes, stacks and cycles with off-weeks, site rotation, GLP-1 brand support, side-effect, nutrition and meal-scan logging, check-ins, a bloodwork tracker, a half-life estimator, PDF reports for clinicians, a 75–90+ compound "Research Library" and the "Pep Bot" AI chat ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6758682710); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.replit.pepai); [pepaiapp.com/reconstitution-calculator](https://pepaiapp.com/reconstitution-calculator); [peptide-stack-tracker](https://pepaiapp.com/peptide-stack-tracker)). It syncs only weight, steps and workouts with Apple Health and Health Connect. Sleep, mood and energy are manual check-ins, and **no source mentions camera vitals, heart-rate or blood-pressure capture, symptom triage or red-flag escalation** ([pepaiapp.com](https://pepaiapp.com/); [App Store GH](https://apps.apple.com/gh/app/pep-ai-peptide-glp-1-tracker/id6758682710)). Pricing is a hard paywall after a 27-step onboarding with a 3-day trial. The plans are **$9.99 a month and an annual plan that has moved between $29.99 and $49.99**, with a $3.99 weekly plan in some snapshots ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/pep-ai-peptide-glp-1-tracker); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6758682710); [AppLlama](https://appllama.io/apps/6758682710/pep-ai-glp-1-peptide-tracker)).

Pep AI's safety stance has a clear seam. The listing says it "does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, treatment recommendations, or dosing instructions" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6758682710)). Its own BPC-157 explainer admits there is no established human dosing ([pepaiapp.com/what-is-bpc-157](https://pepaiapp.com/what-is-bpc-157)). Yet the same site runs SEO pages for a BPC-157 tracker, a BPC-157 mcg-to-syringe-units calculator and an HGH-secretagogue tracker, and it describes Pep Bot both as "never gives doses" and as fielding questions about "dosing windows" ([BPC-157 dose calculator](https://pepaiapp.com/bpc-157-dose-calculator); [BPC-157 tracker](https://pepaiapp.com/bpc-157-tracker); [HGH tracker](https://pepaiapp.com/hgh-peptide-tracker)). Privacy signals conflict in the same way. The vendor says data stays on-device or in iCloud and is never sold, but App Store privacy labels have at times declared Device ID and usage data "used to track you", including for third-party advertising ([App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pep-ai-peptide-glp-1-tracker/id6758682710)). That fits the founder's disclosure that Meta ads doubled revenue from about $80k to $160k a month ([Roberge on X](https://x.com/Cedric_Roberge/status/2065546899885904331?lang=en)). Reviews praise the app's simplicity but complain that it "randomly deletes everything", shows false "overdue" states, crashes and stalls on sync after subscribing, and breaks during iPhone-to-Android migration. One reviewer called it "vibecoded AI slop" ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pep-ai-glp-1-peptide-tracker/id6758682710?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.replit.pepai)).

For PeptiSafe, Pep AI matters for three reasons. First, it is probably already downloadable in Africa: a Ghana storefront page is indexed, although Zimbabwe and South Africa availability is unverified ([App Store GH](https://apps.apple.com/gh/app/pep-ai-peptide-glp-1-tracker/id6758682710)). Second, it is repositioning from "peptide bros" toward mainstream GLP-1 users; its homepage now reads "GLP-1 Tracker App for Shots, Food and Side Effects" ([pepaiapp.com](https://pepaiapp.com/)). Third, its growth engine, which pays over 100 creators by engagement plus Meta ads ([Roberge on X](https://x.com/Cedric_Roberge/status/2065546899885904331?lang=en)), would be close to illegal if copied in Zimbabwe, as the regulatory section shows. Pep AI's weaknesses are reliability, an expensive USD paywall, ad tracking, and no clinical governance. PeptiSafe is designed to beat it on exactly those points.

## Thirty-odd trackers have converged on logging and left safety empty

Outside Pep AI, the field splits into a GLP-1 leader and a fragmented peptide niche. **Shotsy** is the GLP-1 tracker to beat. It claims 34,000+ App Store ratings at 4.8 stars, though its own FAQ also says 54,000, an inconsistency. AppBrain estimates about 130k Android downloads, and Shotsy ranks #59 in US Health & Fitness grossing ([Shotsy](https://shotsyapp.com/free-glp-1-tracker/); [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/shotsy-glp-1-shot-tracker/com.shotsy.app); [TrendApps](https://trendapps.dev/app/ios/6499510249/)). It is also the most localised, with 17 languages ([Shotsy vs Glippy](https://shotsyapp.com/alternatives/glippy/)). Among peptide-specific apps, **PeptidePal** (about 4.8–4.9k iOS ratings) and **Peptide Tracker by Flat Six Labs** (about 1.9–3k ratings, fully free) lead ([LynkDose](https://lynkdose.com/blog/best-peptide-tracker-app/); [Regimen comparison](https://helloregimen.com/blog/best-peptide-tracker-apps-2026)). Regimen, PepTracker, Glapp, Peptide AI, PeptIQ, Protocol and a long tail of 2025–26 launches mostly have hundreds of ratings or fewer. Nearly every "best peptide tracker" ranking is written by a competing vendor, so this report uses those pages only for features and prices.

Core features have converged. Dose logging, reminders, site rotation and side-effect severity scales are free or cheap almost everywhere, and the paywall typically sits on half-life charts, Health sync, unlimited compounds, AI and photos ([Shotsy](https://shotsyapp.com/glp-1-tracker/); [Glapp](https://glapp.io/)). AI comes in two forms. One is a GLP-1 lifestyle companion, such as MeAgain's "Capy" (disclaimed as "not a healthcare provider") or GLP AI ([MeAgain](https://meagain.com/glp-1-questions)). The other is a stack or cycle advisor that in effect prescribes protocols for unapproved compounds: reviewers say Peptide AI "recommended a clear, structured cycle based on their goals", and Smart Peptide Tracker scores stacks on "synergy... evidence quality" ([Peptide AI](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/peptideai-peptide-tracker/id6760374374); [peptidetrackertracker](https://peptidetrackertracker.com/apps/smart-peptide-tracker)). **No app found offers symptom triage or red-flag escalation, counterfeit or batch verification, or double-dose detection.** Only PeptIQ offers a real clinician channel, as a B2B clinic console; a contraindication checker attributed to it by a third party could not be confirmed on PeptIQ's own pages ([PeptIQ clinics](https://peptiq.io/clinics); [peptidetrackertracker PeptIQ](https://peptidetrackertracker.com/apps/peptiq)). Vitals integration is shallow: weight from scales, steps and calories. **No consumer tracker found uses camera rPPG**, although Shen.AI explicitly markets a GLP-1 dose-escalation use case ([Shen.AI weight management](https://shen.ai/use-cases/weight-management)).

## Shen.AI is the right vendor for heart rate, not for face blood pressure

Shen.AI (MX Labs) is the best available fit for PeptiSafe. It is not an accurate blood-pressure meter. Its strengths match a Zimbabwe PWA precisely. Video is processed **entirely on the device or in the browser**, with no video upload ([Shen.AI SDK](https://shen.ai/sdk)). It is the only vendor in the field with public pricing. It ships Android, iOS, Web, Flutter, React Native, Capacitor and MAUI SDKs ([developer.shen.ai](https://developer.shen.ai/getting-started/system-requirements)). Its Medical SDK received **CE Class IIa certification under EU MDR from TÜV Rheinland in July 2026**, with systolic and diastolic BP in scope, and it is registered with ANVISA in Brazil as Class II ([Shen.AI blog](https://shen.ai/blog/now-ce-certified)). The Web SDK published on npm as `@shenai/sdk` v3.2.0 returns heart rate, HRV (SDNN), breathing rate, systolic and diastolic BP, a stress index, BMI and weight. It also returns per-scan quality metrics, including an **expected systolic mean absolute error**, which lets an app suppress a BP value when the SDK itself expects it to be poor. Measurement presets run 30 or 60 seconds ([npm @shenai/sdk](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@shenai/sdk); [Shen.AI SDK examples](https://github.com/mxlaboratories/shenai-sdk)).

Two Web SDK functions change the product strategy. `submitCalibrationReading()` calibrates face BP against a cuff reading. `readBpMonitorPhoto()` / `startBpMonitorScan()` read a blood-pressure machine's screen through the camera ([npm @shenai/sdk](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@shenai/sdk)). Together they let PeptiSafe source blood pressure from a validated cuff, which is far more accurate and avoids the camera-BP accuracy problem entirely. Zimbabwean precedent supports this hybrid: the Nyamukuta maternal AI chatbot in Epworth launched alongside physical BP machines ([Gavi](https://www.gavi.org/vaccineswork/zimbabwe-ai-midwife-making-pregnancy-safer-underserved-women)).

Face BP alone is not good enough to drive safety alerts. Shen.AI's own figures, from 132 adults, are **systolic MAE 10.18 mmHg and diastolic MAE 6.22 mmHg** ([Shen.AI medical device](https://shen.ai/medical-device); [clinical trial report](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/680280c18df8e68403545b30/69442890c0ea75659d0412a9_Shen_AI_Clinical_Trial_Report%20(2).pdf)). That is well outside the cuff-replacement norm of mean error ≤5 ± 8 mmHg. No independent validation and no skin-tone breakdown were found. The skin-tone gap in rPPG is real, though it varies. An audit of 100 rPPG studies found darker skin under-represented, with one classic method's heart-rate MAE rising from about 5.2 bpm on Fitzpatrick I–III to 14.1 bpm on V–VI ([npj Digital Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01973-9)). The gap narrows significantly under moderate-to-high lighting ([Behavior Research Methods 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-026-02953-x)). The most relevant evidence for Zimbabwe comes from Nigeria. A 2026 BMJ Open study of the CE-marked Lifelight app across three Kebbi State hospitals found **systolic hypertension sensitivity of 0.04 overall and 0.00 for Fitzpatrick VI**, and the rural site failed for lack of bandwidth ([PubMed 42331583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42331583/)). Shen.AI should be assumed to carry a similar BP risk on very dark skin until it produces Fitzpatrick V–VI-stratified data. Heart rate is more robust, and **resting heart rate rising across GLP-1 dose escalation** is the defensible signal. Shen.AI's own GLP-1 pitch frames it the same way, as informing rather than replacing the clinician ([Shen.AI weight management](https://shen.ai/use-cases/weight-management)).

### Price, device and licensing realities

Price is Shen.AI's weakest point for an African consumer app. The official pricing page shows **Core at €500 a month with 500 scans included**, or €5,400 a year. Pro and Enterprise are custom, there is a 30-day free trial, and the page contradicts itself on overage (€0.20 vs €0.10 per scan) ([pricing.shen.ai](https://pricing.shen.ai/)). Figures of $750 and €1,500 that circulate elsewhere do not match current sources and appear stale or garbled from G2's $500/$1,000/$1,500 tiers ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/shen-ai/reviews)). At launch volumes, a scan costs about **€1**, which is more than many Zimbabwean users will pay for a month of the app. At 5,000 scans a month the blended cost falls to roughly €0.19–0.28 a scan, depending on which overage rate holds. That makes rationing scans (at onboarding, at each dose step and weekly) and negotiating an emerging-market per-scan rate essential.

Device requirements are the second constraint. Native Android needs Android 8+, a 64-bit ARM CPU, **4 GB of RAM**, OpenGL ES 3.1 and a steady 30 fps camera at 640×480 or better ([developer.shen.ai](https://developer.shen.ai/getting-started/system-requirements)). The Web SDK adds OffscreenCanvas with WebGL 2, WebAssembly SIMD and exception handling. It downloads **roughly 35–40 MB of WebAssembly runtime on first use**, with a ~33 MB threaded build plus private and compute variants. It needs COOP/COEP/CORP cross-origin-isolation headers, though since v3.2 it falls back to a JSPI runtime when isolation is unavailable ([npm @shenai/sdk](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@shenai/sdk); [Shen.AI SDK examples](https://github.com/mxlaboratories/shenai-sdk)). At Econet's roughly US$1/GB, that first download costs users about four US cents ([Business Times](https://businesstimes.co.zw/38223-2/)), which is cheap but noticeable on small bundles and slow connections. It should be prefetched on Wi-Fi and disclosed. Licence activation and model download need the network. Mobile can cache the licence offline "for a limited period" ([developer.shen.ai Android](https://developer.shen.ai/platforms/android)), but the web grace period is undocumented. No data exists on how many Zimbabwean phones meet the 4 GB bar, so the app must capability-check every device and fall back gracefully to manual or cuff-photo entry.

The licence is the third constraint. The SDK is "proprietary software... provided under a commercial Shen.AI agreement", and redistribution or use "outside the scope of that agreement is not permitted" ([npm @shenai/sdk](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@shenai/sdk)). PeptiSafe cannot ship to real users on a trial key and must sign before launch. Questions for that negotiation are whether a web API key can be domain-locked or minted per session, since keys in a PWA are visible to anyone; whether the CE IIa certificate covers the Web SDK build; the length of the web offline grace window; Fitzpatrick-stratified accuracy; and any territory or data-residency terms for Africa. A directory lists Africa among Shen.AI's regions, but no named African customer was found ([healthaidb](https://www.healthaidb.com/software/shen-ai/)). Shen.AI's largest named deployment is Wellx in MENA, with about 500,000 checks in 2025 ([Shen.AI customer story](https://shen.ai/customer-story/turning-daily-engagement-into-preventive-health)), so PeptiSafe would be an early African reference customer. That is negotiating leverage.

## Calculators, camera blood pressure and medicine ads are the regulatory tripwires

The app-store rules bite at exactly the features that make PeptiSafe distinctive. **Apple Guideline 1.4.2** allows drug-dosage calculators only from a manufacturer, hospital, university, insurer, pharmacy or other approved entity, or with regulator approval. **Guideline 1.4.1** rejects apps whose health-measurement accuracy "cannot be validated" and bans claims to measure blood pressure "using only the sensors on the device". **Guideline 5.1.1(ix)** requires healthcare apps to be submitted by a legal entity, not an individual. **5.1.2(i)** requires disclosure and consent before sharing data with third-party AI ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Enforcement shows up in developers' own changelogs rather than in removals. Peptide Tracker, VialWise and Peptide Library removed their calculators to stay listed, one citing 1.4.2 by name, and Pep AI's calculator disappeared and then returned "buried behind another window" ([VialWise](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vialwise-peptide-tracker/id6774017323); [Peptide Library](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/peptide-library-and-tracker/id6755092737); [Pep AI reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pep-ai-glp-1-peptide-tracker/id6758682710?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)). AI health chat apps have been rejected under 1.4.1 even after adding per-reply disclaimers and consent pop-ups ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/807889)). Since 26 March 2026, Apple also requires some health apps to declare medical-device status on their product pages ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/03/26/new-app-store-policy-requires-medical-device-disclosures-for-some-health-apps/)). Google Play bans apps that promote or sell **unapproved substances** "regardless of any legality claims", requires a health-apps declaration from every app, and has enforced a Health and Medical Apps policy since 28 January 2026 ([Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en); [declaration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14738291)).

The PWA route is a real advantage here. Shipping as a web app on an 88%-Android market avoids app-review gating entirely for the Zimbabwe launch. It does not change the underlying medical risk, however. The FDA's July 2024 alert documented patients drawing **five to 20 times** their intended dose from compounded semaglutide vials because of mg/mL/"units" confusion and prescriber conversion errors, some of whom were hospitalised with pancreatitis, gallstones and dehydration ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-alerts-health-care-providers-compounders-and-patients-dosing-errors-associated-compounded)). PeptiSafe's unit-slip detection addresses exactly this failure, and its converter could equally cause it. The converter should therefore verify a prescriber- or label-supplied dose, showing mg, mL and syringe units side by side, rather than generate one.

Software-as-a-medical-device rules converge on the same line. Logging, reminders and education are low-risk self-management, while software that **calculates a patient-specific dose or triages symptoms** is generally a device. Commentators place dose calculation in EU MDR Class IIb–III ([Johner Institute](https://www.johner-institute.com/articles/regulatory-affairs/mdr-rule-11-software/); [Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/ck1t1brmb5c820b235aka2g0e/software-under-the-medical-devices-regulations)). In Africa, **Nigeria's NAFDAC** has required SaMD registration since 1 July 2024 ([NAFDAC](https://nafdac.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/Files/Resources/Guidelines/DR_And_R_Guidelines/Guidelines-for-Registration-of-Software-as-a-Medical-Device-SaMD-in-Nigeria.pdf)). **Kenya's PPB** issued a medical-device-software guideline in 2026 ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2026-04-22-new-rules-set-for-health-apps-medical-software)). **South Africa's SAHPRA** classifies AI-enabled devices as potential SaMD ([SAHPRA MD08](https://www.sahpra.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/MD08-20252026_-SAHPRA-Communication-to-Industry-AI-Medical-devices_Acknowledgements.pdf)), and Ghana publishes a SaMD guideline ([Ghana FDA](https://fdaghana.gov.gh/guideline-for-registration-of-software-as-medical-device)). **Zimbabwe's MCAZ** clearly regulates only condoms and gloves as devices, and its device regulations were still in draft in 2023 ([PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0287495)). That makes Zimbabwe a gap rather than a safe harbour. PeptiSafe's red-flag engine and converter would need registration in Nigeria and Kenya, so it should be built to IMDRF and IEC 62304 discipline from the start. Shen.AI's CE IIa covers Shen.AI's component, not PeptiSafe's app.

Medicine-advertising law is the tripwire most likely to catch a growth team. In Zimbabwe, **advertising any medicine without MCAZ's prior written approval is a criminal offence** under MASCA s.40 and SI 150 of 1991 s.65 ([Pindula](https://news.pindula.co.zw/2024/02/13/advertising-medicines-without-written-approval-from-mcaz-a-criminal-offence)). In **July 2026 MCAZ began engaging influencers and content creators** over medicine promotion on social media ([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202607160534.html)). MCAZ and the Health Professions Authority had already jointly warned against illegal Ozempic sale and use in June 2025 ([The Anchor](https://www.theanchor.co.zw/authorities-warn-against-illegal-ozempic-use/)). In South Africa, Schedule 2+ medicines, semaglutide included, may be advertised only to health professionals, and SAHPRA and the Pharmacy Council stepped up enforcement against unregistered GLP-1s in May 2026 ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-advertising-of-medicines-and-medical-devices/south-africa); [SAHPRA](https://www.sahpra.org.za/press-releases/sahpra-and-the-sapc-crack-down-on-unlawful-manufacturing-of-unregistered-glp-1-medicines/)). US precedent points the same way. The FDA's 2026 warning letters to "research use only" peptide sellers treated the sale of **bacteriostatic water for reconstitution** as evidence of intended human use ([Biotech Research](https://biotechresearch.co.uk/intelligence/fdas-september-2026-peptide-vendor-sweep-five-warning-letters-one-clear-doctrine-on-research-use-only-claims)). The FTC's NextMed order punished unsubstantiated weight-loss figures and fabricated testimonials ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-07-17/html/2025-13407.htm)). The practical rule follows: market the safety tool and never a named medicine, and take no money from peptide vendors.

| Risk | Trigger | PeptiSafe exposure | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple 1.4.2 | Unaffiliated dose calculator | mg→units converter | PWA first; recast as label verification; any store build under a pharmacy or hospital partner account |
| Apple 1.4.1 / Google health policy | Sensor-only BP claim; unvalidated accuracy | Shen face BP | Label BP "estimate"; cuff-photo and calibration as primary path; submit CE documentation |
| SaMD (NAFDAC, PPB, SAHPRA) | Dose calculation, symptom triage | Red-flag engine, converter | IEC 62304 / IMDRF technical file before Nigeria and Kenya |
| MCAZ s.40 / SA Reg 42 | Public promotion of named medicines; paid influencers | Launch marketing | Tool-only messaging; no brand names in ads or ASO; no paid product content |
| FDA intended-use / Google "unapproved substances" | Vendor links, reconstitution supplies, research-peptide protocols | Peptide library | No vendor links or affiliate codes; dated regulatory status per compound |

## Zimbabwe rewards Android, WhatsApp, mobile money and cross-border vigilance

Zimbabwe's market structure favours PeptiSafe's architecture and works against US competitors. Mobile traffic is **88% Android and 12% iOS** ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/zimbabwe)). Internet penetration is about 85% ([Herald](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/data-usage-surge-powers-telecoms-sector-growth/)), data costs about **US$1/GB** ([Business Times](https://businesstimes.co.zw/38223-2/)), and WhatsApp is the most-used platform ([ITWeb](https://itweb.africa/article/more-zimbabweans-engage-social-networks-as-ott-dominates/LPwQ5Ml6lWDvNgkj)). Payments run in dollars and through EcoCash. Estimates put 73–85% of transactions in USD ([Bulawayo24](https://bulawayo24.com/index-id-news-sc-national-byo-261291.html)), EcoCash holds more than 86% of mobile money ([NewsDay](https://newsday.co.zw/business/article/200040539/ecocash-maintains-market-leadership-amid-rising-competition)), and Paynow aggregates EcoCash, OneMoney, InnBucks, O'Mari, Visa, Mastercard and ZimSwitch behind one API ([Paynow](https://www.paynow.co.zw/Home/MerchantTutorial)). Google Play supports Zimbabwean merchants but not EcoCash for in-app digital goods, which historically pushed developers into "illegal payment channels" ([TechZim](https://techzim.co.zw/2018/03/local-android-developers-using-illegal-payment-channels-but-they-might-not-really-have-a-choice-at-the-end-of-the-day)). A PWA with Paynow checkout avoids that conflict. Diaspora remittances reached **US$2.45 billion in 2025**, and the UK has overtaken South Africa as the top source ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-26/zimbabwe-foreign-remittances-rose-14-to-2-45-billion-last-year)). That makes a "UK or SA relative pays for the plan" flow a natural revenue line.

Demand is real but grey and cross-border. Zimbabwe's diagnosed diabetes market is small on paper: the IDF estimates about 106,500 adults (1.5% prevalence) with **69% undiagnosed**, but on survey data from 2005, while other estimates run as high as 10% ([IDF](https://diabetesatlas.org/data-by-location/country/zimbabwe/); [Health Policy Watch](https://healthpolicy-watch.news/zimbabwe-struggle-with-rising-diabetes-and-lack-of-data/)). Ozempic's MCAZ registration status is unverified and disputed ([ozempiczimbabwe.com](https://ozempiczimbabwe.com/); [everyone.org](https://everyone.org/ozempic-semaglutide)). South Africa is the supply hub. Wegovy launched there in August 2025, Novo's authorised copy Extensior launched at 15% below Ozempic in July 2026, Sun Pharma's generic Semagard followed in August at about 18% below, and SAHPRA is reviewing 12 more generic applications ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/08/18/2025/weight-loss-drug-wegovy-launches-in-south-africa); [Business Day](https://www.businessday.co.za/news/health/2026-07-24-novo-cuts-semaglutide-price-15-with-ozempic-rival-launch/); [TimesLive](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-08-25-the-first-wave-of-generic-weight-loss-drugs-hit-sa-in-august-will-it-be-enough-to-kill-the-black-market/); [CNBC Africa](https://www.cnbcafrica.com/2026/south-africa-regulator-reviews-12-generic-semaglutide-applications)). Counterfeit Ozempic has already been reported in South Africa ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/africa/urgent-warning-as-fake-ozempic-infiltrates-south-african-market-m5otk8x7)). Novo launched Ozempic and Wegovy in Kenya in September 2026, and Indian generics as cheap as about $14 are coming ([Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/danish-pharmaceutical-novo-nordisk-introduces-ozempic-and-wegovy-drugs-in-kenya-n390070); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/cheap-weight-loss-jabs-india-set-to-reshape-global-obesity-fight-1.500480837)). PeptiSafe's Zimbabwe user therefore buys semaglutide brands, mostly from South Africa, and switches between them as prices fall, while keeping pens in a hot climate with power cuts. That makes **multi-brand semaglutide support, authenticity checks, brand-switch dose sanity and cold-chain guidance** more valuable than any research-peptide feature. Tirzepatide generics are years away because of patents running into the 2030s ([EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/11/17/ozempic-alternatives-could-weight-loss-injections-soon-become-cheaper-in-south-africa)).

Channels favour partnership over head-on competition. Zimbabwean telehealth is fragmented: KumbaCare, Online Doctors Africa (which integrates Paynow but was not zero-rated at launch), ZimbosAbantu's mobile clinics and 28 ZimSmart Villages telehealth centres all operate without a dominant player ([TechZim](https://www.techzim.co.zw/2025/10/online-doctors-africa-healthcare-credit); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/28/how-telehealth-is-helping-close-the-healthcare-gap-in-rural-zimbabwe)). Any of them could host PeptiSafe's safety and vitals module. WhatsApp-first health works at scale: MomConnect reports more than 4.7 million users with WhatsApp engagement about 10× SMS, but one in four dropped out during registration, and SMS remained necessary for users without data ([Reach Digital Health](https://www.reachdigitalhealth.org/resources/momconnect-chatbot); [BMJ Global Health](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5922483)). For Africa-next, South Africa offers the strongest B2B payer: Discovery's weight-loss drug claims nearly tripled in three years ([BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/business/877884/discovery-sees-claims-for-weight-loss-drugs-nearly-triple-in-three-years/)). Nigeria's Kansei Health already runs doctor-led GLP-1 telehealth from ₦209,000 a month ([Kansei](https://kanseihealth.com/)), making it a potential distribution partner rather than a rival. No sourced data was found on Shona or Ndebele interface use, so localisation priority needs primary user research.

## What PeptiSafe should build and how it should launch

Every competitor has left the same gap: a tracker that acts on what it records. PeptiSafe already has the rarest pieces, including red-flag triage, contraindication screening, double-dose and unit-slip detection, regulator-linked counterfeit verification, cold-chain warnings and WhatsApp clinician handoff. Camera vitals would make it the first consumer GLP-1 tracker to monitor heart rate and blood pressure across dose escalation. Two things decide whether it wins. One is treating the vitals honestly, which makes it trustworthy to clinicians and regulators. The other is distributing it the way Zimbabwe already pays and communicates. The recommendations below are ordered by risk reduced per unit of effort.

The go-to-market follows from the same logic. Launch in Harare and Bulawayo through pharmacists, prescribers and diaspora networks rather than influencers. Frame the message around "check your pen, check your dose, check your heart" without naming a drug. Use the published validation pilot as the credibility asset that a vibe-coded US app cannot copy.

## Conclusion

The competitive picture changes once you look past feature counts. Pep AI and Shotsy have saturated tracking and turned it into a commodity, which means the scarce asset in this category is permission: from regulators, from app stores, and from users who have seen counterfeits and overdoses. Each feature that makes a tracker more "helpful" in the US style, such as dose calculators, AI dosing chat, influencer growth and vendor links, is precisely what Zimbabwe's MCAZ, Apple's 1.4.x rules and the FDA's intended-use doctrine now punish. PeptiSafe's advantage is that its safety-first design and its compliance posture are the same product decision.

The Shen.AI integration should be judged in the same way. Its value is not a blood-pressure number from a selfie. That number is currently least reliable for the very users PeptiSafe serves. Its value is a low-friction, on-device heart-rate trend plus a camera that can read a real cuff. A PeptiSafe that publishes the first African rPPG validation and builds alerts only on what that validation supports would turn the field's shared blind spot into a moat that a better-funded US competitor cannot close quickly.
